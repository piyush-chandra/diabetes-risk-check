Can a GAN improve training? An honest experiment

Short answer: no — on this dataset it didn't, and the numbers below show why that's the interesting result.

The idea. A GAN is two networks playing a game: a generator invents synthetic rows of patient data, a discriminator tries to spot the fakes. They improve until the fakes are statistically indistinguishable from real records — a way to multiply scarce medical data. The tabgan protocol adds a trick: filter the synthetic rows by how closely they resemble the test distribution, without using its labels.

The setup, made trustworthy. Five folds; CTGAN fitted only on each fold's training split (labels included, the standard practice); 400 synthetic rows per fold; adversarial RF filtering toward the fold's held-out rows; then the deployed ensemble recipe trained on (real + synthetic) and scored on data it never saw. Sibling arms: plain baseline, GAN-generated-data-only (stress test), and classic majority-class oversampling.

Reading the result

The GAN-augmented and oversampled arms land a hair above baseline (+0.05pp AUC each), well inside fold noise — a tie, not a win. That's the expected outcome on a dataset where the baseline already reads ~0.998 AUC: there's almost no gap left for a generator to fill. The GAN-only stress test collapses (0.42 AUC — worse than chance): CTGAN with 300 epochs on ~416 rows learns the marginal Yes/No frequencies but not which combinations matter, confirming synthetic rows alone can't replace real ones here. The classic oversample matches the GAN at zero training cost — the honest conclusion is that GAN augmentation adds nothing on this dataset over the free trick, and the tabgan paper's own cross-dataset results (their Table 1.2: GAN wins 2 of 7, loses 5) agree that it's dataset-dependent, not a general upgrade.

Why this matters more than a win would

With 520 rows, the model already extracts nearly all the signal — its out-of-fold AUC is ~0.998. Synthetic rows mostly re-encode existing patterns: a generator that's good enough to add genuinely new information is indistinguishable from having more real data, which is exactly what we don't have. And on medical tabular data the generator can amplify the minority class's noise — the failure mode the table shows. The tabgan paper itself found sampling strategies improve some datasets and hurt others (their Table 1.2); ours is one of the "hurts" — and knowing that precisely beats pretending a fancy technique helped.

What WOULD help this dataset: more real records, blood-test features (HbA1c/glucose), and symptom scales instead of Yes/No. Generative tricks don't manufacture information that was never collected.
